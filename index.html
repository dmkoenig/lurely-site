The problem

Scams have become an industry.

Call centres work from scripts and process thousands of targets a day. The money almost always passes through mule accounts: accounts held by intermediaries that receive victims' funds and move them on within hours. Banks usually spot a mule account after the money has gone.

How it works

Every scam ends with “send it here”. We are there when it does.

A scammer has to tell every victim where to pay. A decoy that plays the victim learns the account before any real money reaches it.

  1. 01 · BAIT

    Decoys go where scammers hunt

    Each decoy persona has its own phone number, life story and way of writing, and appears in the places scammers look for victims.

  2. 02 · ENGAGE

    A believable conversation

    An AI model plays the persona in the scammer's own language, with the doubts, family stories and slow replies of a real person.

  3. 03 · EXTRACT

    Payment details, checked

    Bank accounts, IBANs, crypto wallets, payment-app numbers and websites are captured and validated before they enter the database.

  4. 04 · DELIVER

    Each bank gets its own accounts

    A bank receives the mule accounts held at that bank, with the full conversation attached as evidence.

Why now

Regulators are moving the cost of scams onto banks.

Where a bank pays for a victim's loss, it will pay to stop the loss. Three markets have already made that shift.

  1. October 2024 · United Kingdom

    Mandatory reimbursement

    Banks must reimburse victims of push-payment scams. In 2025 they returned 61% of losses.

  2. November 2025 · European Union

    New payment rules agreed

    When a scammer poses as a bank employee, the bank refunds the full amount. Platforms that ignore reported scam ads become liable to banks.

  3. July 2026 · Australia

    Scams Prevention Framework

    Banks, telecom operators and digital platforms must prevent, detect and disrupt scams, with penalties of up to A$50M.

Principles

Built to be trusted by banks and regulators.

The decoys follow fixed rules in every conversation.

Never pays

No transfers and no fake receipts, ever.

Never shares real data

No real names, card numbers, one-time codes or passwords.

Never starts the contact

Decoys respond to scammers. They do not approach the public.

Steps away when unsure

If the other side does not look like a scammer, the decoy ends the conversation.

Never escalates

No threats, no provocation, no encouragement of other crimes.

Data under control

Stored on our own servers and handled under the law of each country we work in.
